Palazzo Usellini is situated in the historic centre of the town of Arona, on the Piedmontese shore of Lake Maggiore, along Via Pertossi—the historic carriage road that originally led to the town's commercial harbour, active until the early 20th century when it was transformed into present-day Piazza del Popolo. Via Pertossi ascends gently from the lakefront square towards Via San Carlo, where the expansive Monastery of the Visitation stands.

![Piazza del Popolo and the Church of Santa Marta](/storia/piazza-scaled.jpg)
*Piazza del Popolo and the Church of Santa Marta*

The architectural complex as it stands today dates back to the end of the 18th century, specifically to 1783—a date inscribed on several buildings of the property—though its central structural core has far older origins. Dating back to 1530 are the main body facing Via Pertossi, the internal three-sided colonnaded portico, and the doorway that communicates with the ancient Church of Santa Marta across a small inner courtyard.

![The date inscribed on the "Casacce"](/storia/data1783-scaled.jpeg)
*The date 1783 inscribed on the "Casacce"*

From historical maps of Arona, it is difficult to reconstruct the exact characteristics of the structures predating 1783, or to determine whether they originally formed part of the Church of Santa Marta, the adjoining convent, or both. Nor do records survive regarding the initial acquisition of the property by Carlantonio Usellini in the second half of the 18th century.

![Teresian Cadastral Map of Arona (1721)](/storia/Mappa-teresiana-1721_page-0001-2-scaled.jpg)
*Teresian Cadastral Map of the town of Arona (1721)*

Carlantonio Usellini (1741–1816), at the age of 31, requested legal emancipation from his father Filippo and, using his inherited share, left Lake Maggiore for the Netherlands. In Amsterdam, he established an international merchant enterprise that proved exceptionally prosperous. The Usellini family originated from Corciago in the Alto Vergante, with records dating back to Lorenzo Usellini (1636–1710). They were respected landowners across the Novara province (Gattico, Oleggio, Arona, Suno), yet Carlantonio pursued international trade before returning to real estate later in life. In Amsterdam, joined by his brother Pietro Maria, he founded an import-export trading firm dealing in fine European goods: British steel, Flemish textiles, German porcelain, and antiquities. He traveled widely across France, England, and Germany, developing an extensive commercial network well beyond the Dutch market. He often journeyed back to Italy by long-distance stagecoach. In 1792, at age 51, having transferred the thriving company to his brother, he returned permanently to Arona to reside in the palace on Via Pertossi. Historical family accounts recount that he commissioned an unknown Dutch architect to craft a wooden model of the facade, seeking to incorporate forms of the Dutch Baroque—subsequently modified when traditional Dutch sash windows were replaced with classical northern Italian shutters, though surviving sash window frames remain preserved in the internal portico.

Internally, the original 16th-century three-wing portico was preserved, while in the rear garden two independent pavilions were constructed: *“gli Stallini”* (the stables for carriage horses) and *“le Casacce”* (housing the grand ballroom and billiard salon).

Carlantonio appointed the residence with fine European furniture and antiquities, many imported directly from the Netherlands. Following his death, his son Filippo Usellini (1809–1872) administered and expanded the family estates, serving as Mayor of Corciago and later as Mayor of Arona. In 1834 he married Guglielmina Brielli, daughter of Pietro Brielli—Senator of the Kingdom of Sardinia under King Charles Albert and wealthy landowner. Their youngest son, Lorenzo Usellini (1856–1925), inherited the residence in Arona.

Lorenzo Usellini relocated to Milan where in 1882 he founded an enterprise for the manufacture and distribution of fine perfumes and cosmetics, active through the late 20th century. Consequently, the Arona residence served primarily as the family's summer retreat.

Among Lorenzo's children were [Gianfilippo Usellini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gianfilippo_Usellini) (1904–1971), one of the most distinctive Italian painters of the 20th century, whose metaphysical and narrative paintings frequently portray architectural vistas, courtyards, and porticos of his family home; and [Guglielmo Usellini](http://www.ause.eu/public/medias/2008_2_2008_M_MARONGIU.pdf) (1906–1958), a key figure of the European Federalist Movement who served as its Secretary General until his passing in Paris.

Today, Casa Usellini opens its doors during the summer season to host classical chamber concerts and cultural exhibitions, remaining inhabited by the family.
